[FEDERAL-DECREE-LAW-38-2024-1.1]
UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 38 of 2024, Article 1.1 — Short Title
This Decree-Law may be cited as the "Federal Decree-Law on Medical
Products, the Pharmacy Profession, and Pharmaceutical Establishments
(2024)" and shall come into force on 2 January 2025.

---
[FEDERAL-DECREE-LAW-38-2024-1.2]
UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 38 of 2024, Article 1.2 — Definitions
"Establishment" means the Emirates Drug Establishment (EDE), the federal
authority responsible for regulating medical products, including medical
devices, in vitro diagnostics, pharmaceuticals, and herbal products.

---
[FEDERAL-DECREE-LAW-38-2024-2.1]
UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 38 of 2024, Article 2.1 — Registration Application
Application for medical device registration shall include: (a) device
description and intended purpose; (b) risk classification per EDE
Classification Guidelines; (c) Declaration of Conformity (DoC); (d)
technical file; (e) IFU in Arabic and English (Arabic prominence
required); (f) labelling in Arabic and English; (g) clinical evaluation
report for Class III/IV; (h) risk management file per ISO 14971:2019.

---
[FEDERAL-DECREE-LAW-38-2024-2.2]
UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 38 of 2024, Article 2.2 — Local Authorized Representative
Applicant must appoint a UAE-based Local Authorized Representative (LAR)
or Marketing Authorization Holder (MAH) holding a valid EDE Medical
Device Establishment License (MDEL). The LAR/MAH's appointment letter
must be legalized by UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MoFA).

---
[FEDERAL-DECREE-LAW-38-2024-2.3]
UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 38 of 2024, Article 2.3 — Arabic Language Requirements
All patient-facing documentation (IFU, labelling, patient leaflets) must
be provided in both Arabic and English. The Arabic version must be at
least as prominent as the English version, with minimum text height of
1.6mm. The Arabic version must be reviewed by a certified Arabic medical
translator and stamped by the LAR/MAH.

---
[FEDERAL-DECREE-LAW-38-2024-2.4]
UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 38 of 2024, Article 2.4 — Cybersecurity Requirements
Cybersecurity documentation is required for connected medical devices:
(a) cybersecurity risk assessment per IEC 81001-5-1; (b) software bill
of materials (SBOM); (c) threat model; (d) post-market cybersecurity
plan (PMCP). AI-enabled devices are subject to enhanced review per the
DOH Responsible AI Standard (October 2025).

---
[FEDERAL-DECREE-LAW-38-2024-2.5]
UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 38 of 2024, Article 2.5 — Post-Market Surveillance
Post-Market Surveillance System (PMSS) documentation must be submitted.
Must include: vigilance reporting procedure (adverse events within 5
days for death/serious injury), trend reporting (quarterly for Class
III/IV), periodic safety update reports (PSUR) — every 2 years for
high-risk devices.

---
[FEDERAL-DECREE-LAW-38-2024-2.6]
UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 38 of 2024, Article 2.6 — Data Protection
Data protection compliance statement required for devices processing
personal health data. Must reference UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of
2021 (PDPL) and Federal Law No. 2 of 2019 (ICT in Health): (a) data
flow diagram; (b) lawful basis for processing; (c) data retention
policy (minimum 25 years for medical records); (d) data localization
requirement — health data must be stored within UAE.

---
[FEDERAL-DECREE-LAW-38-2024-3.1]
UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 38 of 2024, Article 3.1 — Anti-Monopoly Mechanism
Effective February 2026, every medical device must have multiple LAR/MAH
assigned. The single-distributor model is abolished. Manufacturers must
appoint at least two LAR/MAH with valid EDE licenses, ensuring geographic
coverage of the target emirates.

---
[FEDERAL-DECREE-LAW-38-2024-3.2]
UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 38 of 2024, Article 3.2 — UAE PASS Authentication
All applications to EDE must be submitted via the EDE digital portal
(www.ede.gov.ae) using UAE PASS authentication. The LAR/MAH must have
an active UAE PASS account. Application forms must be signed and stamped
digitally.

---
[FEDERAL-DECREE-LAW-38-2024-4.1]
UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 38 of 2024, Article 4.1 — Fees and Timelines
Medical device registration fee: AED 5,000 + AED 100 application fee.
Renewal fee: AED 250. Registration certificate is valid for 5 years.
Standard evaluation timeline: 45 business days from complete submission.

---
[FEDERAL-DECREE-LAW-38-2024-4.2]
UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 38 of 2024, Article 4.2 — Renewal
Renewal application must be submitted at least 30 days before certificate
expiry. Late renewal: AED 250 + late fee AED 100. Expired certificate
requires full re-registration.

---
[FEDERAL-DECREE-LAW-38-2024-5.1]
UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 38 of 2024, Article 5.1 — Free Sale Certificate
Free Sale Certificate (FSC) from country of origin must be submitted,
legalized by UAE Embassy in the country of origin. FSC must be valid and
not older than 6 months at time of submission.

---
[FEDERAL-DECREE-LAW-38-2024-5.2]
UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 38 of 2024, Article 5.2 — Manufacturer Site Registration
Manufacturers importing to UAE for the first time must register their
manufacturing site with EDE. Site registration is valid for 5 years and
requires ISO 13485:2016 certification. MDSAP (Medical Device Single
Audit Programme) reports are accepted as QMS evidence.

---
[FEDERAL-DECREE-LAW-38-2024-6.1]
UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 38 of 2024, Article 6.1 — SaMD Classification
Software as a Medical Device (SaMD) is classified from Class I to Class IV,
following the same GHTF principles as MD. AI-enabled devices are subject
to enhanced review per the DOH Responsible AI Standard (October 2025).

---
[FEDERAL-DECREE-LAW-38-2024-6.2]
UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 38 of 2024, Article 6.2 — Wearable and AI Devices
Wearable and AI-based devices are explicitly defined as "Medical Equipment"
under this Decree-Law. They are subject to the same registration, labelling,
and post-market surveillance requirements as traditional medical devices.

---
[FEDERAL-DECREE-LAW-38-2024-7.1]
UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 38 of 2024, Article 7.1 — Vigilance Reporting
Adverse events involving death or serious injury must be reported to EDE
within 5 days of awareness. Field Safety Corrective Actions (FSCA) must
be reported within 5 days. The Field Safety Notice (FSN) must be provided
to affected customers.

---
[FEDERAL-DECREE-LAW-38-2024-7.2]
UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 38 of 2024, Article 7.2 — Periodic Safety Update Reports
PSUR (Periodic Safety Update Report) must be submitted for high-risk
devices (Class III/IV MD, Class C/D IVD) every 2 years for the lifetime
of the device. PSUR must include: adverse event summary, trend analysis,
benefit-risk reassessment, and CAPA (corrective and preventive actions).

---
[FEDERAL-DECREE-LAW-38-2024-8.1]
UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 38 of 2024, Article 8.1 — Federal Decree-Law 27/2026
Effective July 2026, Good Pharmacovigilance Practices (GVP) standard
applies to all medical device post-market surveillance. Manufacturers
must maintain a Qualified Person for Pharmacovigilance (QPPV).

---
[FEDERAL-DECREE-LAW-38-2024-9.1]
UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 38 of 2024, Article 9.1 — Multi-Emirate Layer
EDE is the federal authority. However, emirate-level health authorities may
impose additional requirements: Dubai Health Authority (DHA) for Dubai,
Department of Health (DOH) for Abu Dhabi, Dubai Healthcare City Authority
(DHCR) for Dubai Healthcare City free zone. Federal EDE approval is
mandatory for sale anywhere in UAE.

---
[DOH-RAI-2025-1]
Abu Dhabi Department of Health — Responsible AI Standard (October 2025)
AI-enabled medical devices marketed in Abu Dhabi must additionally
comply with: (a) algorithm transparency documentation; (b) bias
assessment report; (c) ongoing performance monitoring plan; (d)
clinician feedback mechanism; (e) patient consent for AI-assisted
decisions. Failure to comply → registration rejection in Abu Dhabi
emirate (still acceptable federally via EDE).

---
[FEDERAL-LAW-2-2019-3.1]
UAE Federal Law No. 2 of 2019 — ICT in Health, Article 3.1
Health information generated or processed by medical devices must be
stored within UAE territory. Cross-border data transfer requires
explicit patient consent and EDE approval. Medical records must be
retained for minimum 25 years.

---
[FEDERAL-LAW-2-2019-3.2]
UAE Federal Law No. 2 of 2019 — ICT in Health, Article 3.2
Health information systems must implement technical and organizational
measures to ensure confidentiality, integrity, and availability of
health data. Encryption is required for data in transit and at rest.

---
[PDPL-45-2021-1.1]
UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (PDPL), Article 1.1
Health data is classified as "sensitive personal data" under the Personal
Data Protection Law. Processing of health data requires explicit consent
from the data subject, except where processing is necessary for medical
diagnosis or treatment provided by healthcare professionals.

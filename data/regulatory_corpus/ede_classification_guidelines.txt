[EDE-CL-GHTF-R1]
EDE Classification Guidelines (2024 update), GHTF Rule 1
Surgically invasive medical devices intended for transient use (less
than 60 minutes) are classified as Class II. Examples: surgical forceps,
retractors, infusion sets, biopsy needles.

---
[EDE-CL-GHTF-R2]
EDE Classification Guidelines (2024 update), GHTF Rule 2
Surgically invasive medical devices intended for long-term use (more
than 30 days) or implantable devices are classified as Class II, unless
they are intended to be implanted in the teeth (Class II) or to contact
the central nervous system or central circulation (Class IV).

---
[EDE-CL-GHTF-R3]
EDE Classification Guidelines (2024 update), GHTF Rule 3
Reusable surgical instruments (devices used in surgery but not in direct
contact with the patient) are classified as Class I.

---
[EDE-CL-GHTF-R4]
EDE Classification Guidelines (2024 update), GHTF Rule 4
Non-surgically invasive devices intended for transient use via body
orifice (e.g. ear speculum, dental dam) are classified as Class I,
unless they are intended for long-term use (Class II).

---
[EDE-CL-GHTF-R5]
EDE Classification Guidelines (2024 update), GHTF Rule 5
Non-invasive devices that come into contact with body fluids (e.g.
collection bags, urine drains) are classified as Class II, unless they
are intended for transient use only (Class I).

---
[EDE-CL-GHTF-R6]
EDE Classification Guidelines (2024 update), GHTF Rule 6
Non-invasive devices intended for delivering substances to the body
(e.g. irrigation sets, drug delivery tubing) are classified as Class II
if the substance is intended to modify or be absorbed by the body.

---
[EDE-CL-GHTF-R7]
EDE Classification Guidelines (2024 update), GHTF Rule 7
Non-invasive devices that come into contact with injured skin (e.g.
wound dressing) are classified as Class I, unless they are intended
for wounds which breach the dermis and cannot be healed by secondary
intent (Class II).

---
[EDE-CL-GHTF-R8]
EDE Classification Guidelines (2024 update), GHTF Rule 8
Non-invasive devices not covered by Rules 5-7 are classified as Class I
(default for non-invasive, low-risk devices).

---
[EDE-CL-GHTF-R9]
EDE Classification Guidelines (2024 update), GHTF Rule 9
Active therapeutic devices that deliver energy (e.g. electrotherapy,
ultrasound therapy) are classified as Class II, unless they are
intended to control, monitor, or directly influence vital body
functions (Class III).

---
[EDE-CL-GHTF-R10]
EDE Classification Guidelines (2024 update), GHTF Rule 10
Active diagnostic devices that monitor vital physiological parameters
where the nature of variations could result in immediate danger to
the patient (e.g. anesthesiology monitors, ICU monitors) are
classified as Class III.

---
[EDE-CL-GHTF-R11]
EDE Classification Guidelines (2024 update), GHTF Rule 11
Software (embedded, not standalone SaMD) used in medical devices is
classified based on the device it controls. Standalone SaMD has its
own classification per Federal Decree-Law No. 38 of 2024 Article 6.1.

---
[EDE-CL-GHTF-R12]
EDE Classification Guidelines (2024 update), GHTF Rule 12
Active devices that administer or remove substances from the body
(e.g. infusion pumps, dialysis) are classified as Class II, unless
the administration is done in a manner that is potentially hazardous
(Class III).

---
[EDE-CL-GHTF-R13]
EDE Classification Guidelines (2024 update), GHTF Rule 13
Active diagnostic devices that emit non-ionizing radiation (such as
magnetic resonance, ultrasound) are classified as Class III. This
includes MRI systems, ultrasound scanners, and diagnostic lasers.
AI-enabled diagnostic imaging software embedded in such devices may
also be subject to SaMD classification per Federal Decree-Law No. 38.

---
[EDE-CL-GHTF-R14]
EDE Classification Guidelines (2024 update), GHTF Rule 14
Active diagnostic devices that emit ionizing radiation (X-ray, CT,
fluoroscopy) are classified as Class III. Required radiation dose
documentation and ALARA (As Low As Reasonably Achievable) justification.

---
[EDE-CL-GHTF-R15]
EDE Classification Guidelines (2024 update), GHTF Rule 15
Devices used for contraception or prevention of sexually transmitted
diseases are classified as Class III, unless they are implantable or
for long-term use (Class IV).

---
[EDE-CL-GHTF-R16]
EDE Classification Guidelines (2024 update), GHTF Rule 16
Implantable contraceptive devices or those preventing STDs are
classified as Class IV.

---
[EDE-CL-GHTF-R17]
EDE Classification Guidelines (2024 update), GHTF Rule 17
Disinfecting or sterilizing devices specifically intended for invasive
medical devices are classified as Class II.

---
[EDE-CL-GHTF-R18]
EDE Classification Guidelines (2024 update), GHTF Rule 18
Devices utilizing animal or human tissues or cells (e.g. collagen
implants, biological dressings) that are non-viable or rendered
non-viable are classified as Class III, unless they are intended to
contact intact skin only (Class I).

---
[EDE-CL-GHTF-R19]
EDE Classification Guidelines (2024 update), GHTF Rule 19
Blood bags are classified as Class II (with anticoagulant) or Class I
(without anticoagulant).

---
[EDE-CL-GHTF-R20]
EDE Classification Guidelines (2024 update), GHTF Rule 20
Devices incorporating or consisting of medicinal substances (e.g.
drug-eluting stents, heparin-coated catheters) are classified as
Class III, unless the substance is a high-risk biological (Class IV).

---
[EDE-CL-GHTF-R21]
EDE Classification Guidelines (2024 update), GHTF Rule 21
Implantable devices that come into contact with the central nervous
system, central circulation, or whose failure could lead to death or
serious deterioration of health, are classified as Class IV. Examples:
heart valves, neurostimulators, vascular stents in central circulation.

---
[EDE-CL-GHTF-R22]
EDE Classification Guidelines (2024 update), GHTF Rule 22
Implantable devices intended for long-term use in teeth, eye, or non-
central body tissues (e.g. breast implants, intraocular lenses, dental
implants) are classified as Class III.
